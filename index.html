Frogs are amazing.

They drink through their skin, freeze solid and thaw back to life, and have been hopping around for about 200 million years. Here is why they deserve your full attention.

Six things frogs can do that you can't

Every one of these is real, documented frog behaviour.

0 sips

They drink through their skin

Most frogs never drink with their mouths. They absorb water through a thin "drinking patch" on their belly and thighs.

−6 °C

Wood frogs freeze solid

In winter a wood frog's heart stops and up to two thirds of its body water turns to ice. In spring it thaws and hops off.

👁 ↓

They swallow with their eyes

When a frog gulps, its eyeballs sink down into its head and help push food towards its throat.

20×

Olympic-level jumpers

Many frogs can leap more than 20 times their own body length. For a person, that would be a jump of about 35 metres.

See-through

Glass frogs have clear bellies

Look underneath a glass frog and you can see its heart beating. While sleeping, they hide most of their red blood cells in their liver to turn almost invisible.

200 Myr

Older than most dinosaurs

Frog-like animals were already around in the early Jurassic. They outlived T. rex by tens of millions of years and are still here.

Frogs need a hand

About two in five amphibian species are threatened with extinction, mostly because of habitat loss, disease and climate change. A few small things genuinely help:

  • Add a pond to your garden, even a small one, with no fish in it.
  • Leave a wild corner with log piles and long grass.
  • Skip slug pellets and garden pesticides.
  • Watch out for frogs and toads crossing roads on damp spring nights.
